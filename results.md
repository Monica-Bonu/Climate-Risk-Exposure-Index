## 2. Regional Patterns

The index reveals a clear regional structure in physical climate hazard exposure.

| Region | Mean Composite Score | Mean Temperature | Mean Precipitation | Mean Drought |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Africa | 0.609 | 0.761 | 0.268 | 0.798 |
| Other anchors | 0.609 | 0.779 | 0.443 | 0.605 |
| Europe | 0.235 | 0.391 | 0.198 | 0.114 |

African countries and the non-European global anchors (India, Australia, Brazil, United States) share the same mean composite score (0.609), both more than twice the European average (0.235). European countries cluster tightly at the lower end of the ranking — 11 of the 14 European countries score below 0.250. The exception is the southern European group: Portugal (0.575), Greece (0.449), Spain (0.393), and Italy (0.359) rank noticeably higher than their northern European counterparts, driven by elevated temperature and drought exposure consistent with Mediterranean climate conditions.

---

## 3. Hazard Drivers of High-Exposure Countries

The driver decomposition reveals that the top-ranked countries reach their scores through different hazard profiles, not a single shared driver.

**India (rank 1, score 0.878)** is the only country to score above 0.9 on two indicators simultaneously — temperature (0.901) and precipitation (1.000). India's extreme monsoon rainfall makes it the highest-scoring country on the precipitation indicator, while sustained heat extremes push it close to the top on temperature. Drought exposure is also elevated (0.735), reflecting regional water-deficit conditions outside the monsoon period. India's composite score is the highest because it is highly exposed across all three hazard dimensions simultaneously.

**Nigeria (rank 2, score 0.785)** is primarily a drought-driven result. Its drought score (0.948) is the second highest in the dataset, reflecting long dry seasons characteristic of the Sahel and Guinea savanna climate zones. Temperature exposure is also very high (0.897), while precipitation exposure is moderate (0.509). Nigeria's ranking is dominated by the combination of extreme heat and severe water deficit.

**Australia (rank 3, score 0.742)** scores the maximum value (1.000) on both temperature and drought — the only country to anchor both indicators simultaneously. Its precipitation score is relatively low (0.227), meaning Australia's exposure is driven entirely by heat and prolonged dry conditions rather than flooding. This is consistent with its predominantly arid and semi-arid interior climate.

**Portugal (rank 4, score 0.575)** and **Brazil (rank 5, score 0.570)** both score moderately across all three indicators (no extreme on any single one), with temperature as the dominant contributor in both cases. Portugal's profile reflects its Mediterranean climate; Brazil's reflects its large tropical and semi-arid interior regions.

---

## 4. Lower-Exposure Countries

The nine countries at the bottom of the ranking (ranks 13–21) all score below 0.250. This group is made up entirely of northern and western European countries — Belgium, Germany, Poland, Netherlands, Norway, Denmark, United Kingdom, Sweden, and Ireland. Their low scores reflect temperate maritime and continental climates with moderate temperatures, no prolonged dry seasons, and moderate precipitation.

Norway is the coldest-running country on the temperature indicator (normalised score: 0.000) and the reference minimum for drought (0.000), anchoring the lower end of both scales. Ireland records the lowest composite score overall (0.074), driven by the lowest temperature extreme (0.020), lowest drought exposure (0.005), and moderate precipitation (0.197).

## 5. Sensitivity Analysis Results

The ranking was recalculated under five alternative weighting schemes to test whether the conclusions depend on the equal-weighting assumption.

| Country | Equal | Temp-heavy | Precip-heavy | Drought-heavy | No drought |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| India | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Nigeria | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Australia | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 |
| Portugal | 4 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 3 |
| Brazil | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 |
| Kenya | 6 | 6 | 7 | 5 | 7 |
| South Africa | 7 | 7 | 8 | 6 | 8 |
| Greece | 8 | 8 | 6 | 8 | 6 |

India and Nigeria hold ranks 1 and 2 under every scheme tested. Australia holds rank 3 in four of five schemes (dropping to 4 when drought is removed, at which point Portugal rises to 3). The top three positions are robust to weighting assumptions. Mid-table positions (ranks 4–8) show modest shuffling of two to three places, particularly for countries that score moderately across all three indicators rather than extremely on one. The overall structure of the ranking — a high-exposure group of tropical and semi-arid countries, a mid-range group of Mediterranean and southern European countries, and a low-exposure group of northern European countries — is stable across all weighting schemes tested.
